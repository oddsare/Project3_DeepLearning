Discussion

Configs 1, 2, and 4 all scored the same BLEU of 0.1500 on the test set, while Config 3 was the only one that clearly underperformed at 0.1000. Looking at the training curves though, Config 4 (more data) was still the strongest — it had a higher training BLEU during development and lower validation loss, which tells me doubling the data helped even if the final test scores are tied here.

Config 3 was clearly the weakest (BLEU 0.1000). LR=0.001 was too slow to converge in 300 epochs, and dropout=0.3 added too much noise for such a small dataset. Too much regularization ends up hurting more than helping here.

All four configs show pretty severe overfitting — training loss drops below 0.2 while validation loss stays above 4.0. Given that we're only working with 600–1200 ten-token sentence pairs, I think that's expected behavior.

Discussion

"go ." → "va !": The attention here is very clean — the model focuses on "go" when predicting "va" and shifts to "." when predicting "!". It's basically a diagonal pattern, which makes sense for such a short, direct sentence.

"i lost ." → "j'ai perdu .": This one is more interesting. The model attends to both "i" and "lost" together when generating "j'ai perdu", which suggests it learned to handle the French contraction as a combined unit rather than a word-by-word mapping.

"he is young ." → "il est <unk> .": "young" didn't appear at least twice in the 600 training examples, so it never made it into the vocabulary (min_freq=2). At inference time it gets replaced with <unk>. Even so, the model still put attention on the right source position, which shows the attention mechanism is working correctly — it just can't output a word it was never taught.

Discussion

On this small dataset, all multi-head variants (1/2/4/8 heads) scored the same BLEU of 0.1500, matching the Bahdanau reference. So multi-head attention didn't hurt, but didn't clearly win either. I think the advantages become more visible on larger datasets and longer sequences where having multiple attention views really matters.

The tie across all head counts is partly a resolution issue — with only 20 test sentences, BLEU scores only change in steps of 0.05. Looking at validation loss, the different head counts do show different overfitting patterns. With hidden size 64, 8 heads gives only 8 dimensions per head which is very tight, so I'd still pick 4 heads as the most balanced choice for this model size.

Part 3: Transformer Architecture (Extra Credit)

Task 1 – Transformer Model Comparison

Transformer ★0.20000.26174.5132
Seq2Seq + Bahdanau (Part 1)0.15000.18815.2953
Seq2Seq + Multi-Head 4h (Part 2)0.15000.20084.8452

Discussion

The Transformer clearly came out on top with BLEU=0.2000, compared to 0.1500 for both Bahdanau and the Multi-Head GRU. It also had the lowest validation loss (4.51), so it's not just memorizing — it actually generalizes better on this dataset.

I think the main reason it works better is that the Transformer's encoder uses self-attention, so every token can directly relate to every other token in one step. With a GRU encoder, information has to travel through sequential hidden states, and that can lose context over longer sequences. The Transformer decoder also benefits from causal self-attention over its own outputs, giving it a richer context at each decoding step.

What surprised me is that the Transformer was already winning even with just 600 training examples and 200 epochs. I'd expect the gap to be even larger on bigger datasets where the GRU's sequential bottleneck would become a more serious limitation.

Task 2 – Ablation Study

ModelBLEUTrain LossVal Loss
Transformer (full baseline)0.20000.26174.5132
No Positional Encoding0.20000.24584.6688
No Self-Attention0.15000.28254.6876

Discussion

No Positional Encoding (BLEU: 0.2000): Removing positional encoding matched the full model exactly. With sequences of only 10 tokens, word order seems recoverable enough from cross-attention alone. The training loss was also slightly lower (0.2458 vs 0.2617), suggesting less overfitting without it. On longer sequences I'd expect positional encoding to become essential.

No Self-Attention (BLEU: 0.1500): This had the clearest negative effect — BLEU dropped from 0.2000 to 0.1500 and training loss was higher and less stable (0.2825 vs 0.2617). Without self-attention, each token in the encoder can't relate to the rest of the sequence within the same layer. This confirms that self-attention is the most critical component of the Transformer, at least at this scale.
